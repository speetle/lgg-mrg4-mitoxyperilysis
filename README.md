## Primary data are not redistributed here

All primary data are public and were retrieved by the authors:

- **TCGA-LGG** — cBioPortal study `lgg_tcga_pan_can_atlas_2018` (RNA-Seq V2 and clinical data).
- **CGGA-325** and **CGGA-693** — Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas, <http://www.cgga.org.cn>
  (RSEM and clinical annotation, release 2020-05-06). The two cohorts share no
  patient (the intersection of their `CGGA_ID` sets is empty), so CGGA-693 is an
  independent replication cohort rather than a re-partition of CGGA-325.
- **Single-cell** — CZ CELLxGENE Discover census, dataset
  `21722308-5091-4b63-9c07-4f116ab9a7b3` (SL040), glioblastoma, grade IV.

`data/` therefore holds **derived** quantities only — per-patient scores, fitted
coefficients, analysis-set logs, calibration and utility summaries. It contains no
raw expression matrix and no controlled-access data.

## Analysis sets and the two models

- **Model A** — 25-gene ridge (L2, lambda = 1.0) Cox index, fitted on the full
  89-gene MRG panel in TCGA-LGG. Apparent C = 0.8211; external CGGA-325 C = 0.6637;
  SD of the linear predictor in TCGA-LGG = 1.062 (n = 502).
- **Model B (MRG-4)** — four genes frozen by LASSO-Cox plus stability selection
  (pi = 0.75): `+0.258164 BMP1 + 0.463556 KIF15 - 0.237887 TRAF3 + 0.479533 PDGFA`.
  TCGA-LGG apparent 0.794; SD = 0.903031; external CGGA-325 C = 0.769
  (n = 172, 90 deaths).

Every per-SD hazard ratio is per one SD of **that model's own score** in that
model's own analysis set.

## Replication in CGGA-693 (new in v1.0.1)

Both coefficient sets were applied unchanged to CGGA-693, WHO grade II–III
(n = 420, 197 deaths; 172 grade II, 248 grade III; 38 patients without an IDH call
are excluded from the adjusted model only).

| Cohort | MRG-4 C | Model A C | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| CGGA-325 (n = 172) | 0.769 (0.722–0.812) | 0.664 (0.610–0.715) | +0.105 (0.054–0.162) |
| CGGA-693 (n = 420) | 0.674 (0.633–0.717) | 0.648 (0.602–0.692) | +0.026 (−0.007 to 0.061) |

The ordering repeats in both cohorts; the *difference* excludes zero in the first
only. The four-gene score itself loses 0.095 (0.049–0.140) between cohorts while
the 25-gene index moves by 0.016, and the two differences differ by 0.079
(0.018–0.141), so the shrinkage is a testable interaction rather than two point
estimates. Within WHO grade III of CGGA-693 the difference is −0.002, which bounds
the advantage near zero in the stratum that carries most of the events.

Discrimination transfers at a lower level and calibration does not: the frozen
baseline over-predicts 36-month survival in both cohorts (predicted 0.831 against
observed 0.660 in CGGA-693, CITL 1.26; predicted 0.853 against observed 0.699 in
CGGA-325, CITL 1.22), and the calibration slope is 0.71 in CGGA-693 against 0.93
in CGGA-325. Calibration slope, CITL, ICI, the Schoenfeld test and the
grade-stratified C-indices are all reproduced by
`code/analysis/transport_diagnostics.py`.

## Reproducing

1. Download the three primary datasets listed above. The CGGA files are
   tab-separated text and need no unpacking.
2. Scripts written before v1.0.1 carry the authors' local input paths at the top
   and are not portable as shipped. The two scripts added in v1.0.1 take their
   paths as command-line arguments instead.
3. Replication and transport diagnostics, end to end:

   ```bash
   python code/analysis/replication_cgga693.py --cgga-dir /path/to/cgga --out-dir replication
   python code/analysis/transport_diagnostics.py --data-dir data --out replication/transport.json
   python code/figures/fig4_second_cohort.py \
       --summary replication/replication_summary.json \
       --transport replication/transport.json \
       --s35 data/Table_S35_calibration_CGGA693.csv \
       --out Figure4_second_cohort.png
   ```

   The first script asserts the published C-indices to 5 × 10⁻⁴ and stops if they
   are not reproduced; the third asserts the six layout invariants of the figure
   and stops instead of writing a misformatted panel.
4. Verify integrity with `shasum -a 256 -c SHA256SUMS.txt` (and, inside `data/`,
   `shasum -a 256 -c SHA256SUMS.txt`).

Bootstrap intervals move in the third decimal with the random seed. The seeds used
for the deposited numbers are 20261111 (replication C-index and its intervals),
20261004 (transport diagnostics) and 20261004 (difference of differences).

Figure scripts for composite panels assume the panels are assembled with a vector
editor; `code/figures/check_*.py` perform the automated overlap, overflow and
occlusion checks reported for each composite figure.

## Scope note

This is a computational re-analysis of public cohorts. The score is a prognostic
association, not a validated clinical test; no wet-lab validation was performed,
and the "mitoxyperilysis-related" label is inherited from the screening panel
rather than from any measurement of lysis. Replication in a second CGGA cohort is
not an independent consortium: both cohorts come from the same resource, and the
limitations section of the article should be read before reusing the score.


## Revision-2 controls (v1.1.0)

Eight scripts added in `code/analysis/` reproduce the controls introduced after
the second full revision round: refit bootstrap (coefficient uncertainty),
out-of-bag and cross-cohort refitting, pooled and within-cohort split halves,
four-horizon calibration with continuous NRI, the parameter-matched null
(4 real genes + 21 permuted columns), and the three-state decision curve.
Their machine-readable outputs are in `replication/analysis_*.json`.

To rerun, point `MRG4_CGGA` at the four CGGA release files described in
`replication_cgga693.py`; the derived tables in `data/` are shipped here:

    export MRG4_CGGA=/path/to/cgga
    python code/analysis/refit_bootstrap.py

The scripts write to `replication/` by default. Override with `MRG4_OUT` and
`MRG4_DERIVED` if the repository is installed read-only.

**Scale caveat.** `Table_S5_CGGA325_MRG4逐患者评分.csv` carries three linear-predictor
columns. Only `MRG4_lp_cohort_z` pairs with the frozen Breslow baseline in
`Table_S19_Breslow基线累积风险.csv`; the other two are on different scales and
must not be used for absolute-risk prediction.
